# The /s/ sound

## What to measure

There is no single number that separates /s/, "th" and "sh" *(expert reply, UTD speech scientist
who studies children's /s/)*. Two pairs matter, and each needs its own measure:

| Pair | What it is | Best measure |
|---|---|---|
| /s/ vs "th" | The common frontal lisp: "thun" for "sun" | **Spectral variance (m2)**, the second spectral moment. A clear /s/ concentrates its noise; a "th" spreads it. Alternative: "degree of sibilance", an amplitude-difference measure from the same researcher's poster (requested, not yet received). |
| /s/ vs "sh" | "shun" for "sun" | **Mid-frequency spectral peak (FM), 3 to 9 kHz**, or center of gravity (m1). In 2.5 to 3.5 year olds, FM predicted listener ratings better than m1. |

Source for the second row: Wong, E. & Munson, B. (2025). Beyond spectral moments: Validating
alternative measures of sibilant fricatives using listener ratings of children's speech. *JASA
Express Letters* 5(10), 105202. doi:10.1121/10.0039497.

## What this changes

The earlier plan was one "center of gravity" bar for /s/. On its own it **misses the frontal
lisp**, the error a screener most needs to catch. The /s/ check needs m2 (or the sibilance
measure) for the lisp and FM or m1 for /s/–"sh".

## Open

- Answered Nov 14 *(expert reply)*: **no fixed cutoff.** Compare each child's /s/ with that child's
  own other sounds, because the values depend on the size of the vocal tract. The /s/ check measures
  m2 within the child, not against an age table. Not built yet.

## Recording rate

A 2026 study reports that 16 kHz recordings are good enough for trained listeners to rate /s/
and /z/ in speakers aged about 8 to 16 (JSLHR, doi:10.1044/2026_jslhr-26-00196). The board
records at 16 kHz. *(Not checked: read from a summary, not the paper.)*
